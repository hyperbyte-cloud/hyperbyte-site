The problem

The problem is tags, not volume.

Time-series databases rarely fall over because of how many points you write. They fall over because of how many distinct series those points create. A database comfortable at a hundred thousand series starts taking seconds to answer queries that took milliseconds.

InfluxDB v1 was built for lower-cardinality workloads, and it is very good at them. Nothing about a real fleet stays low-cardinality. The exits are dropping the tags you needed, downsampling data you will want next quarter, or a tier where replication stops being optional.

High cardinality without the rewrite · The database, on this page

Where the other 98% goes

The storage engine on its own inserts about 99 million rows a second. Put a network, a parser, a series index, and a durable write-ahead log in front of it and you get 1.5 million. Both numbers are true. Only one of them belongs in a capacity plan.

Monitoring, IoT, observability, and analytics stacks already speak InfluxDB v1 line protocol and InfluxQL. HyperbyteDB is a full drop-in: same /write and /query, same clients, written in Rust with columnar MergeTree storage and a RocksDB write-ahead log.

Questions

Asked before the cutover.

Is HyperbyteDB a drop-in for InfluxDB v1?

It speaks line protocol and InfluxQL on the same /write and /query endpoints. Coverage, and the differences, are written up in what works and what does not.

Is self-hosting free?

Yes. HyperbyteDB is Apache 2.0, with no feature gates: replication, sharding, retention, and continuous queries are all in the binary on GitHub. You pay only for the machines you run it on.

What ingest number should a capacity plan use?

1.5 million points a second sustained, p95 under 250ms. That is the number for a capacity plan. Scale-out is linear in the number of shards: add a shard, add that capacity again. Why the engine ceiling is a different number.

How does it scale?

By shards, and the scaling is linear. A Cloud shard is 4 CPU and 16GB of RAM. You start at one shard and add any number after that. Each shard is $299 a month. Disk is $0.20 per GB, on the storage you provision.

What is still ahead for Cloud?

Regions, a published uptime SLA, formal compliance certification, and bring-your-own-cloud. Early access exists so design partners get a say in which lands first. Write to an engineer.
